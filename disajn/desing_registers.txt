### big register flags
- the big register is a lot of registers of type 574, written when the instruction is decoded
- one flag drives a WHOLE register, not one bit at a time, doing it per bit would be wasteful
- registers that have a Hi are moved as full 16 bit by ONE flag, the Hi half rides on bridgeAhi
- the vars are the exception: var1 and var3 sit on bridgeA, var2 and var4 sit on bridgeAhi, and each has its own flag, because they are also used as the low and high half of a 16 bit number
- total: 36 flags, so 5x 574 with 4 spare

ALU Hi toggle
- 0 = bridgeA and bridgeB go into the ADDER, the result is written to ALU out

# Comparator
- a wired block hanging permanently on ALU out and ALU out hi, it is harmless because nothing is written unless the comparison flag write is on
- it always produces three results:
equal = NOR of all 16 bits (all zeros)
smaller = carryAluOut is 0 after a subtraction (a borrow happened)
bigger = NOT smaller AND NOT equal
- bigger is derived, not separately wired, so it can never disagree with the other two
- the three results are gated by the three enable bits in var2 and OR'd together, then inverted into the comparison flag:
result = (bigger AND var2 bit0) OR (smaller AND var2 bit1) OR (equal AND var2 bit2)
comparison flag = NOT result
- because the enable bits are independent, all eight predicates come out of one instruction with no extra opkodes:
1 = >
2 = <
4 = =
3 = != (> or <)
5 = >=
6 = <=
7 = always true
0 = never true
- comparison is done on the FULL result of a subtraction, not on two halves that would have to be merged, so a 16 bit comparison needs no extra held bits


# Bus rules
bridgeA is a BUS
- pull-downs to ground, never holds a value across a takt
- exactly ONE source flag may be on at a time, two sources means two drivers fighting and that kills a driver, not just the result
- zero sources is legal and useful: the bus falls to 0, which is how bridgeB + 1 is done (0 + bridgeB + carry)
bridgeB is a REGISTER
- it holds the RAM address and the adder's B input while bridgeA is doing something else
- this is what makes sub work: the negated number sits in bridgeB while ALU out is being overwritten by the addition


# Timing
- one takt = one ring counter stage
- Q0: decode only, the big register is still all 0 from the last reset, so nothing executes
- Q1..Qlast: the big register drives the taps while the decoder already prepares the next word
- the big register is loaded on the rising edge, the same edge that advances the counter
  at that moment the decoder still holds the previous word, so the right one gets caught
- target registers write in the MIDDLE of the takt (write flags AND inverted clock), never on the loading edge
- n micro steps cost n+1 takts
- the last word carries the reset bit: it executes, then the counter and the big register are cleared and the clock goes back to fetch
